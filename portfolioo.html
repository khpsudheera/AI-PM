01 · End-to-End Product Discovery & PRD 5 Interviews · 28 Survey Responses · Functional Prototype

Instagram: Improving Saved Content Retrieval

An independent product case study investigating why users abandon their Saved libraries as bookmarks accumulate, followed by the design, RICE prioritization, PRD documentation, and prototyping of an in-library multi-attribute search experience.

1. Problem & Core Friction

Users save high volumes of posts and Reels for later reference, but retrieval effort increases exponentially as collections grow. Without search, bookmarks become a "graveyard" of forgotten intent.

2. Proposed Solution

An in-Saved search experience enabling users to query saved content using remembered keywords, caption fragments, hashtags, creator handles, and audio titles.

3. Scope & Execution

Executed 5 user interviews, synthesized 28 survey responses, conducted RICE prioritization, authored a comprehensive PRD (goals, non-goals, user stories, acceptance criteria), and built a functional prototype.

4. Outcomes & Distinction

Actual Work: Research synthesis, PRD documentation, and interactive prototype.
Proposed Impact: Increased Saved item retrieval rate, reduced time-to-retrieve, and lower search abandonment.

Skills in Action

User Discovery & Survey Design
Formulated non-leading questions to uncover saving triggers, organizational habits, and retrieval abandonment patterns.
RICE Prioritization
Evaluated competing user problems against Reach, Impact, Confidence, and Effort to justify Saved retrieval as the primary P0 bet.
PRD Writing & Acceptance Criteria
Drafted product boundaries, non-goals, user stories, edge cases (e.g., partial memory, audio-only recall), and acceptance criteria.
Rapid Prototyping
Built a working interactive prototype demonstrating multi-attribute query parsing and search result interaction flows.

Step 1 — Product & Business Research

Studied Instagram's core loop, business context, and potential friction points across content consumption, followed accounts, and saved collections.

Step 2 — User Research (5 Interviews, 28 Surveys)

Investigated saving triggers, folder usage (only a minority maintain custom collections), and specific scenarios where retrieval was attempted and abandoned.

Step 3 — Insights & Problem Definition

Identified that while saving requires trivial effort (1 tap), retrieval demands high cognitive effort (scrolling through hundreds of unindexed items). Re-evaluated initial assumptions and framed the problem around associative retrieval.

Step 4 — RICE Prioritization

Compared competing validated problems (e.g., auto-categorization folders vs. search vs. reminder notifications). Scored search highest due to high reach across all saving users and direct impact on utility.

Step 5 — Solution Design & Interactive Prototype

Designed search interactions accommodating fuzzy memory: searching by creator handle, caption keywords, audio name, or hashtag.

Step 6 — Comprehensive PRD Development

Documented goals, non-goals, functional requirements, user stories, and acceptance criteria. Defined edge cases such as zero search results and handling expired/deleted original posts.

Step 7 — Success Measurement & Proposed Roadmap

Defined core KPIs: Saved Retrieval Success Rate, Time to Retrieve, and Search Abandonment Rate. Outlined validation phases from internal dogfooding to phased rollout.
